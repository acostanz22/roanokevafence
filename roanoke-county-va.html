What fence work looks like in the county

Subdivision lots usually want a wood or vinyl privacy fence and two gates. Acreage wants farm fence and a gate a truck can use. Cave Spring and Hollins each have their own page because the common job in those communities is not the same.

Clay, rock, roots, and hills show up once the line is walked. They are a cost on both a backyard and a field. A street photo will not show them.

City rules do not cover the county

Roanoke County and the City of Roanoke are different localities. Height, a front yard, and a corner can be treated differently. Salem and Vinton are separate again. Ask the office that covers the parcel, and put the county on the estimate so we plan the height for that lot.

Bring a rough length, the material you are considering, and the gate widths. Say whether the line is a backyard or a field. The estimate names which product the parcel needs instead of forcing one style onto both.

Questions about fencing in Roanoke County

Are county fence jobs priced the same way as city jobs?

The method is the same: length, material, gates, slope, and access. The lots are not the same. A subdivision yard and a pasture are different amounts of fence and different products. We do not use a city backyard price on a long field line.

Do you install both privacy fences and pasture fence in the county?

Yes. Subdivision lots usually want wood or vinyl privacy and a couple of gates. Acreage more often wants farm fence and a gate a truck can use. Both are on the services list. The estimate names which one the parcel actually needs.

Does a county lot follow Roanoke city fence rules?

No. Roanoke County is a different locality from the city. Cave Spring and Hollins sit in the county, not inside the city limits. Height and placement near a street can be treated differently. Ask the county about that parcel instead of assuming a city answer applies.

What should I have ready for a county estimate?

The approximate length, the material you are considering, the number and width of gates, and whether the line is a backyard or a field. Access and rock change the labor, and a street photo will not show them. A look at the line is what makes the price real.
