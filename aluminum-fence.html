Aluminum Fence

Aluminum Fence in Roanoke, VA

Aluminum is the open fence: it marks the yard, keeps a view, and is the material we use most often around pools. It is not a privacy wall, and it can dent.

When aluminum is the right fence

Choose aluminum when you want the space enclosed and the view left alone. Pool yards are the clearest case, because you can see the water and the metal does not rust the way steel does around chlorine.

Skip it when you need a solid screen, or where cars and mowers will hit a thin picket. A dent is the real downside of a light ornamental fence.

Where this fence fits local lots

Choose aluminum when the fence should stay open, including around a pool. Sections can follow a slope more easily than a tall privacy panel.

Materials and styles

The estimate names the height, style, gate count, and anything on the line that changes the dig: slope, rock, trees, and tear-out. We do not publish a flat price per foot.

Repair versus a new run

Sound posts can take a repair. Posts that have failed at the ground usually mean a new line is the clearer job. We will say which one it is after we see the fence.

What an estimate covers

Length, height, style, gates, post method, access, and removal of any existing fence that has to come out. Call (540) 234-2867 or use the form with the town and a rough foot count.

How an aluminum install goes

The gate and the slope matter as much as the picket style.

  1. 1

    Name the job

    Pool, front yard, or a side line that should stay open. A pool fence is priced as a barrier, not as short decoration.

  2. 2

    Walk the slope

    Sections can rack down a hill. A gate still needs a swing that will not bind, so the gate spot is chosen on site.

  3. 3

    Pick the finish

    Black ornamental is the common request. The estimate says aluminum or steel, not a vague metal fence.

  4. 4

    Set and latch

    Posts, sections, and gates go in together. On a pool, the latch and the swing are part of the job.

What changes an aluminum price

Ornamental fence is often less upkeep than steel. It is not automatically the cheapest fence we install. Chain link usually wins that comparison.

Style

A simple picket and a taller ornamental profile do not cost the same. Finials and heavier rails add material.

Pool hardware

A pool gate and latch are specified differently from a garden gate. Say it is a pool on the first call.

Grade

Racking sections to a slope, or stepping them, changes posts and cuts. Steep spots take longer.

Gates and dents

Each opening is hardware. If vehicles swing into the line, we may tell you this profile is the wrong metal.

Aluminum fence questions

Is aluminum a good fence around a pool?

It is the fence we usually recommend for a pool because you can see through it and it does not rust the way steel does around water. A pool fence is also a barrier, so the gate, the latch, and the height have to suit the rules for that locality. Do not price it as a short decorative picket and hope it qualifies. Tell us it is a pool fence on the first call.

Does aluminum rust?

Aluminum does not rust the way untreated steel does. The finish can still scratch, and a deep dent is a real downside of a softer metal. It will not need the paint cycle of a steel fence. We will not say the surface can never chip or mark.

Can an aluminum fence follow a hill?

Yes. Sections can rack to follow a slope more easily than a tall privacy panel. A gate still needs a reasonably level swing, so the gate location is planned on the walk-through. Steep spots may step instead of rack. We agree on that look before we build.

Why pick aluminum instead of a privacy fence?

Pick it when you want the yard enclosed and the view left open. A privacy fence hides the neighbor and also blocks your own view from the house. Aluminum does the opposite. It is a poor choice when the point of the project is a solid screen.

Can aluminum dent?

Yes. That is the tradeoff for a light ornamental fence. A mower, a car door, or a fallen limb can crease a picket. If vehicles back into the line, say so before you choose a thin profile. Chain link or a heavier commercial spec may fit that spot better.

Can you match a black ornamental look?

Yes. Black ornamental aluminum is a common request, including an open picket with finials. It is the look people often mean when they say wrought iron, without the rust of actual iron. If the job should be steel, the estimate will say steel. It should not say a vague metal fence.
