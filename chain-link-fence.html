Chain Link Fence in Roanoke, VA

Chain link is the practical enclosure: a yard, a dog run, a ball field, or a commercial lot. It does not pretend to be a privacy wall unless you add slats or a screen.

Request an estimate Call (540) 234-2867

When chain link is the right fence

Choose it when you need a boundary that is sturdy and see-through, and when privacy is not the point. Homes use it for dogs and side yards. Businesses use a heavier version for lots and storage.

Skip bare chain link if the goal is to block a view. Slats or a windscreen can help, and they also catch wind. A wood or vinyl privacy fence is the direct way to screen a backyard.

How a chain-link install goes

Terminal posts and tension do the work. The fabric is only as good as those posts.

  1. 1

    Confirm the use

    Dog run, backyard, field, or commercial yard. The use picks the height and how heavy the pipe should be.

  2. 2

    Choose the fabric

    Galvanized silver, or a black or green coating. Powder coat is another finish on some products.

  3. 3

    Count the extras

    Gates, slats, and windscreen belong on the first estimate. They are not a small add-on after the fabric is stretched.

  4. 4

    Stretch and hang

    We set terminal posts, stretch the fabric, and hang gates so the gate is not what holds the tension in the line.

What changes a chain-link price

Length and gates are most of it. The ground and the finish are the rest.

Length

More fabric and more line posts. A long, open run is still simpler than a short run full of corners.

Gates

Each gate adds posts and hardware. A double drive gate is a different price from a walk gate.

Coating and height

Silver galvanized is the base. Black or green coating, and a taller fence, cost more.

Layout

Slope, rock, and a yard the truck cannot reach change the labor even when the fabric price looks simple.

Chain-link questions

How does chain-link pricing work?

Most of the price is the length of the fence and the number of gates. Height, a color coating, slats, and a windscreen add to that. The ground still matters: slope, rock, and access change the labor even on a simple fabric. We keep the estimate to those items instead of a single teaser rate.

Can I get black or green chain link instead of galvanized silver?

Yes. Standard fabric is zinc-coated steel, which reads as silver. Black or green usually means a polymer coating over that steel, and some products are powder coated instead. A thicker coating generally holds up longer than a thin one. We price the finish you actually want.

Is chain link only for commercial property?

No. It is a normal residential fence for a dog run, a side yard, or a backyard when you do not need privacy. It is also used on a commercial lot, a storage yard, or a ball field. The gauge, the height, and the fittings should match that use. A light backyard fence is not the spec we put on a commercial yard.

Can you add a gate, privacy slats, or a windscreen?

Yes. Gates are part of almost every chain-link job, and they move the price. Slats or a windscreen add screening that bare fabric does not have, and they also catch more wind. Say if you want them at the start. Adding them later means a second visit and hardware the first estimate did not include.

Will chain link keep a dog in the yard?

It can, if the height matches the dog and the bottom stays close to the ground. A determined digger will go under a fence that sits on soft soil. The gate latch matters as much as the fabric. Tell us the dog and we will price a height and a bottom that fit, without promising that every animal will stay put.

What is different about commercial chain link?

Commercial runs are usually taller, with heavier fittings, and the gates are built for trucks or steady foot traffic. Privacy screen or a barbed-wire top is a site spec, not the default on a house. We install those when the property calls for them. We do not put razor wire on a residential backyard.
