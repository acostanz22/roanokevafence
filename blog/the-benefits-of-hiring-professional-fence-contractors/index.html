The Benefits of Hiring a Fence Contractor

Hiring someone to install a fence is about who lays out the line, sets the posts, and hangs the gates. This note is for a Roanoke homeowner comparing a crew with a weekend project.

Published May 25, 2023. Updated October 16, 2026.

What you are paying the crew to handle

The hard parts are the layout and the posts. A fence that looks straight in a photo still has to follow a yard that drops, miss a root, and meet a gate that has to swing. The crew brings the posts, the panels or rails, and the tools to set them.

You should not have to rent an auger to discover the corner is rock, then rebuild the corner on Sunday. That discovery is part of the job you are hiring.

What to know before the visit

Measure the run as best you can, even if the number is rough. Count the gates, including a wider one for a mower or a driveway. Decide whether the fence is for privacy, a dog, a pool, livestock, or simply a line you want marked.

Confirm the property line yourself. We set the fence where you tell us. We do not survey. On a tight lot in Roanoke or Vinton, that confirmation matters more than the catalog photo.

Repair versus a new line

A few broken boards or a sagging gate can be a repair, if the posts are still solid in the ground. Posts that are soft at the soil, or a line that leans for most of its length, usually mean a new run. New boards on rotten posts follow the lean.

A contractor should say which one it is after looking. A full replacement is not the automatic answer, and a patch is not a favor if it fails next season.

Materials are not interchangeable

Vinyl skips the stain cycle, and many privacy panels look finished on both sides. Wood can be stained, and it weathers if you ignore it. Chain link encloses a yard or a lot and does not give privacy unless you add slats or a screen. Aluminum keeps the view and is the usual pool fence. Farm fence is for animals and long lines. Commercial work specifies the pipe and the gates for traffic, not for a backyard.

A usable price is a layout

Anyone can quote a low number per foot and then add the slope, the gates, and the tear-out. Ask what is included: old fence removal, haul-away, every gate, and whether the posts are set so the line will stay put. We do not list a dollar amount on this site, because the visit is what makes the number real.
