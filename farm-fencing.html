Farm Fencing in Roanoke, VA

Farm fence is a long line with a job: hold horses, hold cattle, or keep animals out of a crop. The product follows the animal. A backyard privacy panel is the wrong default.

Request an estimate Call (540) 234-2867

When the line should be farm fence

Choose it for pastures and long edges where a privacy fence would be a wall nobody needs. Horses get a fence they can see, such as split rail or pipe and cable. Cattle are more often held with barbed wire or electric fence.

A wood farm fence in the open weathers faster than a privacy fence sheltered by a house. Some people want that aged look. Others should pick a product that can stay unfinished or already colored.

How a farm fence is planned

The animal, the gate, and the ground come before the style name.

  1. 1

    Name the animal

    Horses, cattle, or a field that has to keep animals out. Those are different fences, even if they look similar from the road.

  2. 2

    Walk the long line

    Wet spots, woods, rock, and the place a truck has to pass. A map is not a substitute for that walk.

  3. 3

    Match the product

    Split rail, pipe and cable, rail with mesh, barbed wire, or electric, based on what the fence has to hold.

  4. 4

    Build the gate for the traffic

    A farm gate has to clear a truck or a tractor. The hinge posts are sized for that leaf, not for a yard gate.

What changes a farm fence price

On a long run the footage matters, and so do corners, braces, and whether the truck can drive the line.

Length and corners

More posts, more brace assemblies, and every change of direction. A straight field is cheaper to build than a line that jogs.

The animal

Horse rail, cattle wire, and a crop fence with mesh are different materials. The wrong one is not a small swap.

Gates

A wide equipment gate, with posts that can carry it, costs more than the field fence on either side.

Ground and access

Rock, wet ground, and woods slow the posts. A line the truck can drive goes faster than one we carry in.

Farm fence questions

What fence should I use for horses?

Horses need a fence they can see, so they are less likely to run through it or catch a leg. Split rail and pipe-and-cable are the horse options we install. Barbed wire is a poor horse fence. Tell us the horses are there before we price a cattle fence onto a horse pasture.

What fence should I use for cattle?

Cattle are often held with barbed wire or electric fence, which is a different build from a horse rail. A crop field that needs animals kept out is different again, and a rail with mesh is a common choice there. We need to know whether the fence is holding animals in or keeping them out. Those are not the same products.

How do you price a long farm run?

Length dominates, then corners, gates, and how hard the line is to reach. A straight run the truck can drive is simpler than a line through woods, wet ground, or rock. Brace posts and a farm gate wide enough for equipment are separate from the field fence. We walk it before we give a number.

Will a farm fence need more upkeep than a backyard fence?

Often, yes, because it stands in the open. A wood farm fence weathers faster than a privacy fence partly sheltered by a house and trees. Some owners want that silvered look. Others should choose a colored product or a wood that can stay the color it is. An open line in Virginia weather asks for more attention than a shaded yard fence.

Can you put in a gate wide enough for a tractor or a truck?

Yes. A farm gate is part of the plan, not a walk gate stretched wider. Tell us what has to pass and from which side it should swing, or whether it should slide. The hinge posts have to be built for that weight. A light line post will not carry a heavy gate.

Can you fence a pasture that is not level?

Yes. Farm lines on rolling ground are normal around Roanoke. The fence follows the grade, and low spots are where animals find a gap if the bottom is left high. We look for those spots on the walk. Rock and wet ground change how the posts go in, and they belong on the estimate.
