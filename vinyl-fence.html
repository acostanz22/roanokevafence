Vinyl Fencing in Roanoke, VA

When vinyl is the right fence

Choose vinyl when you want privacy or a picket line and you do not want a fence that needs stain. It is a common replacement for wood that has turned into a maintenance chore.

It is a weaker choice on a long farm boundary, where rail or wire costs less per foot, and when you want a fence you can repaint a new color every few years.

Where this fence fits local lots

Choose vinyl for a privacy or picket fence you do not want to stain. A typical privacy panel looks finished from both sides, which helps on a shared lot line.

Materials and styles

The estimate names the height, style, gate count, and anything on the line that changes the dig: slope, rock, trees, and tear-out. We do not publish a flat price per foot.

Repair versus a new run

Sound posts can take a repair. Posts that have failed at the ground usually mean a new line is the clearer job. We will say which one it is after we see the fence.

What an estimate covers

Length, height, style, gates, post method, access, and removal of any existing fence that has to come out. Call (540) 234-2867 or use the form with the town and a rough foot count.

How a vinyl install goes

The visit decides the style and the steps. The build itself is posts, panels, and gates.

  1. 1

    Name the style

    Privacy, picket, or ranch rail, plus the height and the color. Ranch rail is a two-, three-, or four-rail perimeter fence, not a screen.

  2. 2

    Walk the line

    We look at the slope, the gate openings, and whether panels need to step. A flat catalog panel will not follow a Roanoke hillside by itself.

  3. 3

    Price the whole run

    Footage, gates, and any wood fence that has to come out are on the estimate before we dig. There is no separate surprise for the layout after the holes are started.

  4. 4

    Set posts and hang panels

    Posts go in, panels go up, and gates get hung so they latch. We check the bottoms against the grade before we leave.

What changes a vinyl price

Vinyl usually costs more to install than a basic wood privacy fence. These are the items that move the number.

Style and height

A solid privacy panel, a picket, and a ranch rail do not use the same amount of material. Taller privacy costs more than a low picket.

Gates

Every gate is hardware, a post pair, and time. A wider gate costs more than a single walk gate.

Slope and steps

Stepped panels, extra posts, and cuts on a hill take longer than a level run of the same length.

Tear-out and access

Hauling off an old wood fence, or carrying panels through a narrow side yard, is labor the footage alone does not show.

Vinyl fence questions

How much more does vinyl cost than wood?

Vinyl usually costs more to install than a basic wood privacy fence of the same length. Wood stain and board repairs over the years are the costs vinyl is meant to avoid. The gap depends on the style, the gates, and how heavy the wood fence is. We price both from the same layout so you can compare one job.

Does a vinyl privacy fence have a good side and a bad side?

Many vinyl privacy panels look finished from both yards, with no exposed rail side. That helps when the fence sits on a shared line and neither neighbor wants the frame. Picket and ranch rail are open styles, so there is no solid face to assign. Ask which profile you are pricing.

Do you install vinyl picket and ranch rail, or only privacy panels?

We install privacy panels, picket, and ranch rail. Ranch rail comes in two, three, or four rails and does not screen the yard the way a privacy panel does. Picket is the open front-yard look, and privacy is the solid backyard fence. Name the style when you call so the estimate matches it.

Does vinyl need to be painted?

No. Vinyl is chosen because it does not take stain or paint the way wood does. Dirt can still be washed off, and a finish can scratch, but there is no yearly coating to keep the material sound. That is not a promise that the fence will never mark or fade.

Can vinyl panels follow a sloped yard?

Tall privacy panels usually step down a slope so each panel stays plumb. Forcing a flat panel to twist with the hill opens a gap at the bottom or racks the frame. The walk-through is where we decide how the steps land and where a gate can still swing. Slope is a price item, not a surprise after the holes are dug.

When should I skip vinyl?

Skip it on a long pasture, when you want to change the color with stain later, or when the job is a commercial chain-link spec. Vinyl is a yard fence. It is not the default for cattle, a storage yard, or a pool that needs an open view of the water.
