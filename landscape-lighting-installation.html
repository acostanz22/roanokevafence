Wood Fencing in Roanoke, VA

Wood is still the privacy fence most people picture: boards, posts, and a gate. We build privacy, board-on-board, dog-ear, picket, and horizontal board fences, and we repair runs whose posts are still sound.

When a wood fence is the right job

Choose wood when you want a fence you can stain to the house, or a picket or dog-ear line that fits an older yard. Board-on-board covers the rails better than a single-face privacy fence.

Wood is a weaker choice if nobody will maintain it. Virginia sun and rain gray bare boards, and rot at the soil line is what usually ends a neglected wood fence.

How a wood fence gets built

We ask for the footage first, then the layout, then the style. Those three items are the price.

  1. 1

    Pick the build

    Privacy, board-on-board, dog-ear, picket, or horizontal boards. Height and spacing are part of that choice, not a detail we guess.

  2. 2

    Check what is standing

    On a repair we test the posts. Solid posts can take new boards. Soft posts mean the repair will lean with them.

  3. 3

    Separate the extras

    Stain, height, gates, and tear-out are their own lines. A low number per foot that hides the gates is not a usable estimate.

  4. 4

    Hang the finished face

    We set or reuse posts, then boards, with the finished face on the side you chose. Gates get hung so they clear the grade.

What changes a wood fence price

Length is the first question. Style and the ground are the next two.

Footage

A longer line uses more posts and boards. We need a rough measurement even if you have not pinned every corner.

Style

Picket, standard privacy, board-on-board, and horizontal boards do not consume the same lumber. The style is a price choice.

Layout

Level ground, a slope, trees, roots, and rocky soil change post holes and how the boards step.

Access and gates

A yard the truck cannot reach takes longer. Each gate, and any old fence we haul away, is added to the footage.

Wood fence questions

How do you price a wood privacy fence?

We start with the length, then the style, the height, and the gates. A dog-ear privacy line, a board-on-board line, and a picket fence are not the same amount of lumber. Slope, rocks, roots, and a narrow side yard change the labor. Tear-out of the old fence is its own line if it has to come down.

What wood styles can you build?

The styles we build are privacy, board-on-board, dog-ear, picket, and horizontal board. Height, board spacing, and the finished face change the look inside those styles. If you have a photo of a specific build, bring it to the estimate. We would rather match a picture than invent a style name.

How does Virginia weather treat a wood fence?

Sun and rain gray unfinished boards, and the soil line is where rot usually starts. A fence you stain, and keep out of standing water, lasts longer than one that is ignored. We will not put a year-count on a wood fence. Maintenance is part of owning it, which is why some people switch the next one to vinyl.

Can you replace part of a wood fence?

Yes, when the posts are still solid. Boards, a cap, or a gate can be replaced without rebuilding the whole run. If the posts are soft or the line leans for most of its length, new boards will follow that lean. We look at the posts before we call it a repair.

Which face of a wood privacy fence looks finished?

A standard privacy fence has a smooth board face and a rail face. Board-on-board hides the rails better from both sides. Tell us which direction the finished face should point before we hang boards. We build the side you specify.

Is a picket fence enough if I want privacy?

No. A picket fence is an open border. It marks the yard, but people can see through it. Privacy means solid or overlapping boards tall enough to screen the yard. If privacy is the goal, price a privacy style, not a picket.
